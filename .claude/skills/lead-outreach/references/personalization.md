# Personalization at Scale

Personalization drives 50 to 250% more replies (Lavender). The rule that matters: **if the
personalized line has nothing to do with the problem you solve, it is just an attention hack and it
will not convert.**

> **House rule.** Examples below show the shape. Draw personalization from the
> lead's `Signal` and `Hook` fields in Airtable plus one real detail you can verify (their site, a job
> post, their stack). No invented observations. No client names in the proof. No em dashes in the draft.

## The "So what?" test

After writing the opening line, read it as the prospect: "So what? Why would I care?" If the honest
answer is nothing, rewrite it. If deleting the opener leaves the email still making complete sense,
the personalization is not doing its job.

## Four levels of personalization

### Level 1 — Basic (merge tags)
First name, company name, job title. Table stakes, not differentiating. ~5% lift.

### Level 2 — Industry / segment
Industry-specific pain, trend, or regulatory pressure. Scales through micro-segmentation.
> Most {{industry}} teams still move quotes around by email and spreadsheet, which is where the days go.

### Level 3 — Role-level
Challenges specific to their role and seniority.
> As Head of Ops with a lean team, you are probably the one unblocking approvals all day.

### Level 4 — Individual (the goal)
A specific, timely observation about that person, tied to the problem you solve.
> Noticed you are hiring three coordinators. Scaling that team usually means the manual steps scale with it.

## Research signal stack

Maps to what `/lead-find` writes into the `Signal` field, plus anything you can add by hand.

| Signal | Where to find it | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Recent funding | Crunchbase, LinkedIn, press | "Scaling the team from here usually creates [X]" |
| Job postings | LinkedIn Jobs, careers page | "Hiring 3 coordinators sounds like the manual work is growing" |
| Tech stack | BuiltWith, Wappalyzer | "Saw you are on HubSpot, most teams at your stage hit a ceiling with [X]" |
| LinkedIn activity | Posts, comments, job changes | "Your post about [topic] lined up with something I keep seeing" |
| Company news | Google News, press releases | "Congrats on the acquisition, integrating the two ops teams usually creates [Y]" |
| Podcast / talks | YouTube, podcasts | "Caught your point on [X], quoting their own words gets replies" |
| Website changes | Manual review | "Your new pricing page caught my eye, curious how it is converting" |

## The four -graphic dimensions (Becc Holland)

- **Demographic** — role, background, tenure
- **Technographic** — stack and tools
- **Firmographic** — size, funding, industry, growth stage
- **Psychographic** — values, beliefs, what they are passionate about (highest-impact)

## Observation-based openers (highest performing)

**Trigger-event:** "Congrats on the round. Scaling from here is the fun part, and I imagine [challenge] is already on the list."

**Observation:** "Your post about [topic] stuck with me, especially [detail]. Got me thinking about how that plays out in [their function]."

**Industry insight:** "Most [role]s I talk to lose [X hours a week] to [problem]. Curious if that matches your experience at [Company]."

## What reads as fake (avoid)

- AI phrasing: "I hope this email finds you well", "I came across your profile", "leverage", "synergy"
- Attention hacks with no link to the problem ("cool that you went to UCLA" then a pitch)
- Over-personalizing to the point of creepy
- "I saw your LinkedIn and wanted to reach out" signals mass automation

## The 3-minute system (30 Minutes to President's Club)

1. Keep a stack of your top buying signals, 5 company triggers and 5 person triggers, ranked by relevance.
2. Build a 3-part block: personalization tied to a problem, the problem you solve, one-sentence solution + low-friction CTA.
3. Pre-write one segue paragraph per trigger. The rest of the email stays constant. A personalized draft then takes under 3 minutes.
