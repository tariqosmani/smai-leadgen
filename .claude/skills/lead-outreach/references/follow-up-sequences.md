# Follow-Up Sequences

55% of replies come from follow-ups, not the first email. Yet ~48% of salespeople never follow up once.

## How many: 5 total (1 + 4 follow-ups)

- 4 to 7 email campaigns hit ~27% reply rates vs ~9% for 1 to 3 emails (Woodpecker, 20M emails).
- After the 4th follow-up, response drops sharply and spam complaints climb.
- Cap at 4 follow-ups. Each one must add genuinely new value, not "bump this up".

## Cadence (the gap grows each time)

| Touch | Day | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 initial | 0 | Maximum personalization investment |
| Follow-up 1 | +3 | Waiting 3 days lifts response by up to 31% |
| Follow-up 2 | +7 | Different angle |
| Follow-up 3 | +14 | New value piece |
| Follow-up 4 | +24 | Breakup |

**Best days:** Tuesday to Thursday (Thursday peaks). **Best times:** 9 to 11am or 1 to 3pm in the
prospect's local time. **Avoid:** Monday mornings, Friday afternoons.

## Angle rotation (one new value proposition per email)

| Email | Angle | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 initial | Personalized hook + core value + soft CTA | Introduce the problem and the fix |
| Follow-up 1 | A different angle: a new stat, insight, or resource | Show another benefit |
| Follow-up 2 | The closest portfolio build and what it did | Build credibility |
| Follow-up 3 | A new insight or industry trend relevant to them | Show you think about their world |
| Follow-up 4 | Breakup: acknowledge the silence, leave the door open | Trigger loss aversion |

Adding exactly one new value prop per email forces the angle to change on its own.

## The breakup email

Removes the pressure while creating a little scarcity by withdrawing. Cold prospects reply to these at
10 to 15% (Close.com).

**Shape:** acknowledge you have reached out a few times -> accept that the timing may be wrong -> say
this is the last one for now -> leave the door open with one useful line.

> Have not heard back, so I will assume the timing is not right. One thing before I close the loop:
> [a single concrete insight or a link that is useful even if they never reply]. If that changes
> things, just reply. Otherwise no hard feelings, good luck with [their goal].

**1-2-3 format** (near-zero friction):

> Since I have not heard back I will keep it simple. Reply with a number:
> 1, interested, let's talk. 2, not now, check back in 3 months. 3, not interested, stop.

**Rule:** if you send the breakup, honor it. No further contact.

## Phrases that kill response rates

- "I never heard back" -> ~12% drop in meeting-booking rate (Gong)
- "Just checking in" -> zero value, reads as lazy
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox" -> presumptuous
- "Did you see my last email?" -> guilt-trip
- "Following up on my previous message" -> generic, adds nothing

## CTA by seniority

- **Executives / founders:** ultra-low effort, curiosity-driven. "Curious?" "Worth 2 minutes?"
- **Mid-level managers:** more specific value. "Want me to walk through how the intake automation was built?"

Higher in the org chart, less friction you can ask for.
