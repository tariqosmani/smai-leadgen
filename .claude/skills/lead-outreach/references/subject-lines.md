# Subject Line Optimization

The subject line decides whether the email gets read. The data is counterintuitive: **short, boring,
internal-looking subject lines win decisively.** Its only job is to get the email opened, not to sell.

## Length: 2 to 4 words

- 2-word subjects get ~60% more opens than 5-word (Lavender).
- Going from 2 to 4 words drops replies by ~17.5%.
- 2 to 4 words yield ~46% open rates vs ~34% for 10 words (Belkins, 5.5M emails).
- Mobile truncates around 30 to 35 characters, so brevity is also practical.

## Internal camouflage

Subjects that look like they came from a colleague, not a vendor, roughly double open rates (Gong).
Buyers categorize before they open. If it looks like sales, it is filtered.

Good: `reporting load` · `onboarding ops` · `quote turnaround` · `carrier emails` · `second page` ·
`Q2 forecast` · `new patients` · `follow-up gap`

## Capitalization: lowercase wins

All-lowercase has the highest open rate (Gong, 85M+ emails). It reads as personal and internal. For
cold outreach, lowercase beats title case.

## Personalization: context, not name

- **First name in the subject -> ~12% fewer replies.** It signals automation.
- **Context works:** a pain point, a competitor, a trigger event, an industry challenge.
- Use the situation ({{painPoint}}, {{trigger}}), never {{firstName}}.

## Questions: only if highly specific

Specific pain questions work ("need help with [that specific thing]?"). Generic questions fail ("quick
question?", "have 15 minutes?"). Default to a statement.

## Avoid

| Anti-pattern | Impact |
|---|---|
| Salesy words ("increase", "boost", "ROI") | ~-18% opens |
| Urgency ("ASAP", "urgent") | opens fall below 36% |
| Excess punctuation ("!!!", "??") | ~-36% opens |
| Numbers and percentages in the subject | ~-46% opens |
| Emojis | hurt B2B credibility |
| Pitching the product in the subject | ~-57% replies |
| Spam triggers ("free", "guarantee", "act now") | deliverability risk |
| Em dash | house rule, never |

## C-suite

Executives get 300 to 400 emails a day and decide in seconds, but they reply ~23% more often than
others once an email passes the filter. What works: ultra-concise, human, understated. A reference to
a specific project or a trigger event. `an update` · `a question` · `[their initiative]`. Anything
that smells of sales is rejected on sight.

## Default

Touch 1: 2 to 4 words, lowercase, names their situation or a pain, not the product or their name.
Touches 2 to 5: reply in the same thread, keep the subject.
