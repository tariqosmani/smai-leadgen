# Cold Email Copywriting Frameworks

Frameworks beat templates. They teach thinking patterns, not copy-paste shortcuts. Pick one that
fits the lead before you write. Do not freewrite a cold email.

> **House rule for Smart AI Workspace.** The example emails below show STRUCTURE, not copy to reuse.
> They name invented companies and invented results. Every Smart AI Workspace draft follows the voice
> rules in `docs/outreach-playbook.md`, cites only real proof from `references/portfolio.md` (no client
> names, no invented numbers, "built and shipped X" not "helped Y hit Z"), and carries no em dashes.
> Take the shape from these frameworks and fill it with something true.

## Picking a framework

| Lead looks like | Use |
|---|---|
| C-suite / founder, wants brevity | QVC, Mouse Trap, or Justin Michael Method |
| Strong trigger in the `Signal` field (funding, hiring, news) | SCQ, PPP, or Trigger-based AIDA |
| Services pitch anchored on a real portfolio build | 3C's |
| Problem-aware, not solution-aware (the common case) | PAS (default) |
| Clear before/after transformation | BAB |
| Analytical buyer (ops, finance, technical) who needs evidence | ACCA |
| Multi-touch sequence, complex service | PASTOR spread across touches |

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution (default)

**Structure:** Identify pain -> amplify the consequences -> present the solution + soft CTA.
**Best for:** Problem-aware but not solution-aware prospects. The workhorse.

> Most VP Sales at companies your size spend 5+ hours a week on manual CRM reporting. That is 250+
> hours a year not spent coaching reps, and it usually means the forecast reaching leadership is
> already stale. We built a tool that generates those reports in real time. Would it make sense to see how?

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

**Structure:** Current painful situation -> ideal future -> your work as the bridge.
**Best for:** Transformation-driven offers with a clear before/after. Emotional decision-makers.

> Right now your team is probably spending hours a week sourcing leads by hand, feast or famine each
> quarter. Picture qualified leads landing daily on their own, reps spending their whole day selling.
> That is what the system does. Can I show you how it is put together?

## QVC — Question, Value, CTA

**Structure:** Targeted pain question -> brief value -> direct next step.
**Best for:** C-suite prospects who prefer brevity. Qualifies interest immediately.

> Are your SDRs spending more time researching accounts than talking to them? We automate the research
> so reps stay in conversations. Worth a 10-minute look?

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Structure:** Hook or stat -> address a specific challenge -> proof or outcome -> clear CTA.
**Best for:** Data-driven prospects, higher-ticket pitches with a strong stat.

> Manual outreach quietly loses a third of inbound leads before anyone follows up. Given the growth
> you posted about this quarter, pipeline velocity is probably on your mind. We build the qualification
> and routing layer that closes that gap. Worth 15 minutes?

## PPP — Praise, Picture, Push

**Structure:** Genuine, specific compliment -> how things could be better -> gentle push.
**Best for:** Senior prospects who respond to relationship-building. Needs a real trigger.

> Your piece on scaling an ops team without scaling headcount was sharp, especially the part about
> approvals being the hidden bottleneck. What if those ran themselves? Open to a short chat about how
> that would look for your stack?

## Star-Story-Solution

**Structure:** Introduce a character (a build you shipped) -> the challenge -> the result.
**Best for:** A strong build story. Humanizes the pitch.

> A logistics operator was rekeying every carrier email into their TMS by hand, hours a day, errors
> creeping in. We built a parser that reads the email and writes the load straight into the system.
> Happy to walk through whether the same shape fits what you are dealing with.

## SCQ — Situation, Complication, Question

**Structure:** Current reality -> the complicating challenge -> a question that speaks to the need.
**Best for:** Consultative selling. Mirrors how professionals brief leadership.

> Your team doubled this year. That usually means onboarding is eating into selling time. How are you
> handling ramp for the new hires right now?

## ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

**Structure:** Contrarian hook -> explain the benefit simply -> proof -> strong CTA.
**Best for:** Analytical buyers who need evidence (engineers, CFOs, ops leaders).

> Most teams measure how many emails a rep sends. The ones pulling ahead measure how much of the rep's
> week is actually spent in front of a buyer. Automating the busywork is how you move that number.
> Worth seeing how?

## 3C's (Alex Berman)

**Structure:** Compliment -> case study -> CTA.
**Best for:** Agency and services cold outreach. The build does the heavy lifting.

> Been following what you are doing at [Company]. We recently built an intake-to-CRM automation for a
> team in your space that cut their response time from a day to minutes. I have a couple more ideas for
> your setup. Interested?

## Mouse Trap (Lavender / Will Allred)

**Structure:** Observation + a binary value-prop question. One or two sentences total.
**Best for:** Maximum brevity. An impulsive reply driven by curiosity.

> Looks like you are hiring three ops coordinators. Would it help to see which of that work could be
> automated before you backfill all three?

## Justin Michael Method

**Structure:** Trigger or pain -> solution hint -> binary CTA. One to three sentences, no intro.
**Best for:** High-velocity outreach, mobile-optimized. Deliberately direct.

Spend a minute, not ten, on personalization. Use industry and persona-level signals. For top-tier
prospects, quote their own words from a podcast or talk. They almost always reply.

## Vanilla Ice Cream (Lavender)

**Structure:** Observation -> problem/insight -> credibility -> solution -> call-to-conversation.
**Best for:** A universal base when nothing more specific fits. Five short parts.

## PASTOR (Ray Edwards)

**Structure:** Problem -> amplify -> story -> testimony -> offer -> response.
**Best for:** Longer or multi-email sequences. Each element can be developed across separate touches.
